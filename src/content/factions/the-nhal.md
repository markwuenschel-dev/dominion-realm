Every other power in the cosmos *disagrees about what reality should become.* The
N'hal erase the premise that reality can stay coherent enough to become anything
at all. They are not an empire, not an army, not a rival civilization. They are
ontological predators.

They do not conquer worlds. They **denature** them. Near the N'hal, names detach
from the things they named, places remain visible but stop being locatable, oaths
go static, gods lose their address, and the conditions that let anything *mean*
anything begin to rot. For the walkers who carry an implant, the interface itself
degrades — skills, levels, mastery, the stat bars and notifications stop meaning
what they read, because those are translation-structures, and structure is exactly
what the N'hal unmake. What is left is instinct and whatever a person can do
underneath the readout.

This is why a hard-won lesson — *the interface is not the world* — is not a theme
but a survival skill. The Xyloryn are the known apocalypse everyone prepares for.
The N'hal are what almost no one understands exists.
