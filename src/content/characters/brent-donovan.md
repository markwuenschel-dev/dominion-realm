Brent is a civil engineer by trade and by temperament — the man who asks the two
questions that actually matter under pressure: *what is holding this up, and what
makes it come down?* He turns abstract problems into practical constraints, and
he has no patience for elegance that won't bear weight.

In a group of people prone to spiraling, he is the floor. When Marcus disappears
into recursive analysis, Brent cuts through it with plain language and forces him
back into the present, into the thing that can actually be done next. Their banter
is fast and unceremonious, and it works.

He is no one's crafting NPC. Brent is combat-capable and survival-relevant — the
kind of competence that keeps a party alive precisely because it refuses to be
impressed by anything that isn't load-bearing.

## Useful is the safest thing to be

Brent experiences usefulness as the safest form of belonging — if he is holding
the thing up, no one sends him away. Which is exactly why his deepest, least-spoken
need is the opposite: *choose me when nothing is broken.* He is angrier than the
group expects, too, and the anger has a specific trigger — a harm that was
preventable, an entitlement behind it, and a cost quietly transferred onto someone
who didn't agree to carry it. That's not temper. That's the shape of an old wound:
a family, an estate, and a betrayal executed entirely on paper, which is why
abstraction that hides who actually pays makes him see red.

In the Realm his interface is **Piezoelectric Bones** — mechanotransduction. He
reads strain and load in the structures around him, and under stress his own body
remodels to bear more. He grounds every room he stands in, and now the ground can
read exactly how much weight is on it.

## The one the system can't rewrite

Brent is separated on arrival and spends a long stretch alone before he integrates
with a different group — not the Iron Vultures, and the pairing is still unresolved.
His oddness is his edge: his power acts on *matter and structure*, not on the
abstract system-layer the others live inside — which makes him the one enemy the
interface-adapting threats can't simply out-read. At the finale, while everyone
else is fighting an interface, Brent reads the **Myrmidon** as a *structure* — load
paths, failure points, what holds it up and what brings it down. He survives Book
One; the Reckoner he's becoming is further down the road.

</RevealGate>
