Seb ran a top-tier guild, and not just in name — he held the roster together,
made the hard calls on composition and strategy, and managed the personalities
that would have fractured under anyone else. He is genuinely good at it:
strategic, persuasive, responsible, the kind of leader people listen to because
his presence implies competence before he says a word.

What he notices first is the *cost* of a plan. Where Marcus sees the broken
assumption, Seb sees the sacrifice — what a decision spends, who carries it, and
whether the people involved can actually bear it. In the Realm that talent makes
him one of the most formidable of the scattered guild, not the least.

He and Marcus are the same wound expressed as opposite strategies. Both are
high-agency. Both fear helplessness. Marcus answers it by building something real
with people and letting the uncertainty be bearable; Seb answers it by managing
every variable and making every sacrifice worth it. His danger begins the moment
responsibility quietly becomes authority — when carrying the cost starts to feel
like the right to decide what everyone else must pay.

## What leadership costs him

Leadership is both Seb's burden and his validation. He carries the weight because
someone has to, and the only praise that reaches him is the kind that means
*I trust you.* The risk is that the burden curdles: responsibility hardening into
authority, sacrifice quietly reclassified as moral credit, until the man who
carries the most believes he has earned the right to spend the rest. Underneath it
all sits a grief he doesn't put down — a brother running out of time.

In the Realm his power is **Pyric Blood**, and it is merciless in the most literal
way: it burns his own body to keep him functioning past the point where he should
have collapsed. A power that runs on self-consumption is a dangerous gift for a man
who already believes the leader is the one who pays.

## The price of getting him back

Seb's brother dies during the Day-0 scrim — *before* he ever signs Astria's
coercive offer. He goes into the Realm anyway, out of grief, with nothing left on
Earth to stay for. His solo road is the shortest and the ugliest: taken, worked as
forced labor, and out the other side by killing his captor with his bare hands.

Then a cross-world **Broker** of the Soulkeepers' Exchange finds him with the one
offer he cannot refuse to hear: the Broker holds his brother's soul, and the
restoration is *real*. The price isn't paid in coin — it's paid in what Seb has to
*become* to earn it. Book One leaves him standing in front of a noble-framed source
of power, having just rationalized the first warning sign. He does not die. The
turn, the fall, and the collision it sets up with Marcus are still ahead of him.
