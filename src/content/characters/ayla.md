Ayla arrives as a voice in Marcus's head — an ancient celestial consciousness
that attaches to his implant after he survives something he should not have. She
is not an angel on his shoulder and not an exposition machine. Her memory is
fragmented, her knowledge incomplete, and her patience for being treated like a
search engine is thin.

She mentors the way a good teacher does: by refusing to hand over the answer. She
helps Marcus ask the better question, names the place his reasoning broke, and
says less than she knows — often with dry contempt for how slowly he gets there.

Inside one skull she is the counterweight to everything that promises him
certainty. Where other forces offer Marcus confident, false answers, Ayla offers
uncertainty he can actually use. She gives better questions. She does not solve
his life for him.
