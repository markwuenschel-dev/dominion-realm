She arrives as a voice before she is anything to look at — dry, sharp, and older
than the language she borrows to be understood. What image there is comes later:
a remnant of star-pale light, larger than a basketball and nothing like a person,
with no face to read and no wings to soften it. She is not an angel on his
shoulder and not an exposition machine. She is a fallen apex-being worn down to a
fragment, slowly recovering memory and self, and thoroughly unimpressed by how
long her new tenant takes to arrive at the obvious.

Her full name, **Illyristranthe**, was not built for human mouths, and the
awkwardness is the point. **Illyri** is the short form she allows. **Ily** is the
one close friends earn — she hates it, then hates that she has stopped hating it.

She mentors the way a good teacher does: by refusing to hand over the answer. She
names the place a line of reasoning broke, helps Marcus ask the better question,
and says less than she knows — her knowledge is real but fenced by damaged memory
and deliberate omission. Inside one skull she is the counterweight to everything
that promises him certainty. Where other forces offer confident, false answers,
Illyri offers uncertainty he can actually use.

The tether runs both ways, and neither of them chose it. It began in mutual
survival and is only slowly becoming something like friendship — proud, dependent,
humiliated by the dependence, and testing every one of her words for what she left
out. She reads the others with the same unsentimental clarity: an old envy of
Serra's freedom to simply *act*, a wary respect for Mara's habit of treating
omission as a move, and a quiet alarm at the shape of the danger gathering around
Seb.

**What she was, and what it cost.** Illyri found the hidden shape of the thing —
the cosmic game underneath the Realm — far too early, and was defeated and
discarded for it: thrown *outside* reality precisely because she could not be
cleanly erased. She came back the only way left to her, through Marcus's death,
and it was not free. She spent a permanent portion of herself to pull him back —
which is why his resurrection is not the ordinary kind, and why she is now a
fragment rather than a power.

Her arc runs discarded → anchored → accepted → restored, and its cruelty is the
last turn: she recovers enough of herself to leave — an incarnate body that
presents at about twenty-five, wings, the halo relic-organs, the Star-Glaive, and
the celestial battle-magic that channels through them: illumination and
revelation, enhancement, debilitation, stellar offense, stabilization — and then
**chooses the group anyway.** She recognizes the *shape* of erasure when it moves
again — the absence the N'hal leave behind — without yet recognizing the thing
itself. What she is to the Eyes of Meszkhal, and they to her, she will not say.
