Serra came from nowhere in particular and made her name *mean* something — no
connections, no shortcuts, just social intelligence sharpened young into a
survival tool and competitive mastery clawed out one match at a time. She is the
warm center of a group, the person everyone leans toward. People feel her before
they understand why.

In a fight she is entry. Where others hesitate at the edge, Serra has already
chosen her line through — reading the seam in a formation and committing past the
point a careful player would bail. Not reckless. She is betting her instinct is
faster than their reaction, and she is usually right.

Her power makes that gift physical and then makes it costly: emotional commitment
becomes pressure, and pressure finds whatever is weakest and makes it yield. The
harder she fights for something, the more the field presses on everyone near her.
She is most dangerous, and least controllable, precisely when she cares most —
which is the shape of her whole life made visible. She is warm. She is decisive.
And she does not ask anyone to stay.

## Warm on the surface, guarded underneath

The radiance is real, and it is also a door she keeps her hand on. Serra is
selectively open — generous with warmth, careful with the inside — because an
early lesson in misplaced devotion taught her what it costs to hand someone the
whole of you and be handed back less. So she made a rule of it: *I don't ask
people to stay.* Better to leave first than to need. It reads as strength, and it
is; it is also the wound wearing armor.

In the Realm her competitive instinct becomes a named thing — the **Severance
Pulse Interface**, which runs aura into pressure into a clean cut. The warmth
that makes people lean toward her is the same force that, weaponized, finds the
weakest bond in a formation and severs it. With Marcus she builds a genuine tandem
across an early stretch of the Realm — the charge from Earth still unaddressed,
now sharpened by survival — decisive and protective and unwilling to say what it
would cost her to stay.

## The one who leaves first

Serra separates from the party early and goes her own way. She kills a magistrate
running a namebinding outpost — and learns, too late to comfort her, that he was a
*node* and not the source; she leaves with a fragment of his registry and a bigger
map than she wanted. Her solo road is anchored by **Vessa**, a thirteen-year-old
who mirrors the girl Serra used to be, and it is the girl, more than the fight,
that changes her.

At the Book One finale her interface finally ignites under emotional pressure. In
the two movements of the climax she brushes off how close Marcus came to dying,
then is *seen* — involuntarily, wholly — by his newly-awakened eyes. Being read
that accurately is the one thing her whole life is built to prevent. So she does
what she always does, faster than anyone can argue: she severs the bond herself,
rather than ask him to stay — and he doesn't follow. She survives Book One. She is
gone, and the Earth thing is still unaddressed.
