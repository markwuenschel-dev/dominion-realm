**Interface fantasy** is the genre where a character meets a real, irreducible
world through a layer that translates it — a HUD, an implant, a system of menus
and numbers — and the story lives in the gap between the translation and the
truth.

It borrows the furniture of LitRPG: levels, skills, loot, stat bars. But where
much of LitRPG treats the interface as the world's actual operating system,
interface fantasy treats it as a *lens* — useful, legible, and lying by
omission. The numbers are real to the one who reads them and false to the world
they describe.

In *The Dominion Realm*, Marcus's implant renders a metaphysical reality as RPG
logic so a fragile mind can survive contact with it. The book is about what that
lens leaves out — and what it costs to finally see past it.
