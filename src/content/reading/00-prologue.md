summary: Before the Realm, there was a chair, a needle of light, and a question no recruiter would answer honestly — what happens to a mind that is too good at pretending the world is a game.
---

There is a particular silence that lives inside the best players, and Marcus Vye had spent eighteen years cultivating his.

It was not the silence of an empty room. It was the silence behind the eyes when the noise of a thing falls away and only its structure remains — the moment a board stops being wood and ivory and becomes pure consequence, the moment a raid boss stops being spectacle and becomes a sequence of windows, each one a half-second wide, each one a door. Coaches called it focus. His mother had called it *somewhere else.* The recruiters had a different word, and they had flown him across an ocean to use it.

"Legibility," the woman said. Her badge read only **ASTRIA — OBSERVATION**, which told him nothing, which was probably the point. "That's what we measure. Not reflexes. Not memory. The rate at which a person turns chaos into rules they can act on. You score higher than anyone we've tested."

"People say that like it's a compliment," Marcus said.

"It isn't one." She did not smile. "It's a specification."

---

They put him in a chair that cost more than the building his family rented, and they lowered the light toward his face slowly, the way you approach something that might bolt. It was not really a light. It was the tip of it — a filament finer than a thought, and behind that filament, he understood without being told, a quantity of intention he could not see the edges of.

A neuroquantum lattice, the paperwork had called it, in the one paragraph he'd been allowed to read before they took the pages back. Threaded along the optic nerve. *Non-destructive,* the paragraph promised, twice, the way a sentence only promises a thing twice when the first time didn't take.

"You'll feel a cold," the woman said. "Then you'll feel that you've always felt it. That second part is the implant agreeing with you. Don't fight it. The ones who fight it are the ones it has to convince, and convincing is the part that hurts."

"And if I'm not convinced?"

For the first time, something moved behind her face that might have been honesty. "Then you'll be the most interesting result we've ever had," she said. "Briefly."

---

The cold came. He had been braced for pain, for the violence of a thing entering him, and instead it was almost tender — a coolness sliding up the back of his sight like the first held breath before a dive. And then, exactly as promised, the cold was simply *his,* an old companion, something he had carried since childhood and only now remembered owning.

The room rendered.

That was the only word for it. The walls did not change, and yet the walls acquired *meaning* — edges he could read, distances that announced themselves as numbers at the corner of his attention, a soft persistent legibility laid over everything like frost over glass. The chair beneath him reported its own solidity. The woman across from him acquired a thin pale bar above her head, the way a name acquires a face, and the bar was full, and he knew with a certainty that arrived already finished that the bar was her *life,* and that he was not supposed to be able to see it.

"There," she said, and now she did smile, and he hated that the smile looked relieved. "What do you see?"

Marcus opened his mouth to answer.

He never did. Because the floor was already gone — not falling away, nothing so dramatic, simply *replaced,* the laboratory peeling back from his vision like a loading screen that had only ever been there to keep him calm while the real place finished assembling itself underneath. Cold air. The smell of stone and green water and something burnt a long time ago. A sky the wrong color over mountains that no recruiter had mentioned, because no recruiter had known, because the paperwork had lied in the gentlest place of all: it had told him where he was going.

It had not told him he would arrive somewhere *else.*

Above the edge of his sight, calm as a cursor, four words assembled themselves out of nothing and waited for him to be able to read them.

**Welcome to the Realm.**

And beneath that, smaller, the way the truly important things are always smaller:

*This is not a game. It only knows how to talk to you like one.*
